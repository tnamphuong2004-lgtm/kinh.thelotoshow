The History of Lo To: Vietnam's Most Exciting Folk Game

From moonlit courtyards to neon-lit stages - trace the captivating 200-year journey of lo to across Vietnamese culture and society.

By KINH! TeamMay 15, 20268 min read

Lo to is far more than just a game - it is a living piece of Vietnamese cultural heritage that has captivated audiences for over 200 years. Often compared to Western bingo, lo to is a uniquely Vietnamese experience that combines the thrill of chance with theatrical performance, community bonding, and cultural expression. From its humble origins in the courtyards of southern Vietnam to the dazzling neon-lit stages of Ho Chi Minh City, lo to has evolved into one of the most exciting and beloved folk games in Vietnamese culture.

In this article, we trace the fascinating journey of lo to through the centuries, exploring how this simple number-calling game became a cornerstone of Vietnamese entertainment and why it continues to thrive in the modern era - including its latest reinvention through KINH! Lo To Show, a bold new game set that brings lo to into the homes of a new generation.

Key Facts About Lo To

  • Lo to originated in southern Vietnam in the early 19th century
  • The game uses 90 numbered tokens and specially designed cards
  • Traditional lo to is performed with singing, comedy, and theatrical flair
  • Lo to stages were once the primary entertainment in rural Vietnamese communities
  • Today, lo to is experiencing a cultural revival among young Vietnamese people

The Origins of Lo To: How It All Began

The exact origins of lo to remain somewhat shrouded in the mists of history, but most historians agree that the game first appeared in southern Vietnam during the early 1800s. The name "lo to" is believed to derive from the Italian word "lotto," suggesting a possible connection to European lottery games that may have been introduced during the colonial period. However, Vietnamese communities quickly made the game entirely their own, infusing it with local traditions, performance art, and communal spirit.

In its earliest form, lo to was a simple affair. Villagers would gather in courtyards under the moonlight, and a caller would draw numbered tokens from a bag, singing out each number in a melodic chant. Players would mark their cards, and the first person to complete a row would shout the winning call. But even in these early days, lo to was never just about winning - it was about the experience of coming together as a community.

The Golden Age of Lo To: Stage Performances and Travelling Troupes

By the mid-20th century, lo to had evolved from a quiet courtyard pastime into a full-blown theatrical spectacle. Travelling lo to troupes began crisscrossing southern Vietnam, setting up elaborate stages in marketplaces, fairgrounds, and temple festivals. These were not simple game sessions - they were immersive entertainment experiences that could last for hours.

The lo to caller became the star of the show. Far from merely reading numbers, callers were expected to sing, dance, tell jokes, and perform dramatic monologues between draws. Many callers developed celebrity status in their local regions, known for their unique vocal styles, comedic timing, and flamboyant costumes. The best callers could hold an audience of hundreds in rapt attention for an entire evening.

"Lo to is not just about the numbers. It is about the voice of the caller, the gasp of the crowd, the moment when your heart races as you wait for that final number. It is pure Vietnamese magic."

What Made Lo To Stages So Special?

A traditional lo to stage was a feast for the senses. Bright lights illuminated the caller's platform. Music played between rounds. Food vendors lined the perimeter, selling street food to hungry players. Children ran between the rows of seats, and entire families would attend together - grandparents, parents, and children all united by the excitement of the game.

The lo to cards themselves were works of art. Hand-printed on thick paper with bold numbers and colourful borders, each card was unique and often became a treasured keepsake. The numbered tokens were typically made from wood or bamboo, stored in a cloth bag that the caller would shake dramatically before each draw.

Early 1800s

1950s - 1970s

The golden age of lo to. Elaborate stage performances featuring singing callers, comedy acts, and theatrical productions draw massive crowds.

1980s - 1990s

Lo to faces decline as modern entertainment options emerge. Many travelling troupes disband, but dedicated communities keep the tradition alive.

2000s - 2010s

Cultural preservation movements spark renewed interest in lo to. Documentary films and media coverage introduce the game to younger audiences.

2020s - Present

Lo to experiences a full cultural revival. Modern brands like KINH! Lo To Show reimagine the game with contemporary design, bringing lo to into homes across Vietnam and the world.

The Cultural Significance of Lo To in Vietnamese Society

To understand why lo to has endured for over two centuries, you must understand its role in Vietnamese community life. In a culture that places enormous value on family bonds, communal gathering, and shared experiences, lo to provided the perfect vehicle for bringing people together.

Lo To as Community Bonding

In rural Vietnam, where entertainment options were limited, lo to nights were the highlight of the social calendar. They were occasions for neighbours to reconnect, for families to celebrate, and for communities to strengthen their bonds. The game created a shared emotional experience - the collective anticipation, the groans of near-misses, the explosive joy of a win - that bound people together in ways that few other activities could match.

Lo To and Vietnamese Performance Art

Lo to also played a crucial role in preserving and promoting Vietnamese folk performance traditions. The callers' singing drew from traditional Vietnamese musical styles, including cai luong (reformed opera) and various folk song traditions. Many talented performers who might otherwise never have found a stage discovered their calling through lo to troupes. The game became a training ground for entertainers and a showcase for Vietnamese artistic expression.

Lo To and Inclusivity

Perhaps most remarkably, lo to stages have historically been spaces of radical inclusivity. Lo to troupes have long been known for welcoming performers from all walks of life, regardless of background. This tradition of acceptance and celebration of diversity remains one of the most beautiful aspects of lo to culture and continues to resonate powerfully with modern audiences.

The Decline and Revival of Lo To

Like many traditional art forms, lo to faced significant challenges in the late 20th century. The rise of television, cinema, and later the internet drew audiences away from live lo to performances. Many travelling troupes could no longer sustain themselves financially, and the art form seemed in danger of fading into memory.

But lo to proved resilient. Beginning in the 2010s, a wave of cultural awareness swept across Vietnam, driven partly by social media and partly by a growing desire among young Vietnamese to reconnect with their heritage. Documentaries about lo to troupes gained millions of views online. Viral TikTok and YouTube videos showcasing the energy and artistry of lo to performances introduced the game to an entirely new generation.

Today, lo to is experiencing its most exciting chapter yet. The game is no longer confined to stages and fairgrounds - it has entered living rooms, party venues, and social gatherings across Vietnam and in Vietnamese diaspora communities worldwide. How to Play Lo To: A Complete Beginner's Guide

Never played lo to before? No problem. This illustrated step-by-step guide will have you shouting "KINH!" within minutes - everything you need to know about Vietnam's most exciting folk game.

By KINH! TeamMay 17, 202610 min read

Lo to is Vietnam's most beloved folk game - a thrilling combination of luck, performance, and communal joy that has been bringing families and friends together for over 200 years. Often compared to Western bingo, lo to is a uniquely Vietnamese experience that transforms a simple number-matching game into an unforgettable social event filled with singing, laughter, and nail-biting anticipation.

Whether you have just discovered lo to through the KINH! Lo To Show game set or you are curious about this iconic Vietnamese tradition, this complete beginner's guide will walk you through everything you need to know - from the basic rules and equipment to advanced tips for hosting the perfect lo to game night.

Quick Facts: Lo To at a Glance

  • Players: 2 to 50+ (the more the merrier!)
  • Age range: 6 years and up - perfect for all generations
  • Playing time: 15-60 minutes per round
  • Equipment: Lo to cards, 90 numbered tokens, a bag, and a caller
  • Skill level: No experience needed - learn in under 5 minutes
  • Best for: Family gatherings, parties, Tet celebrations, and game nights

What Is Lo To? Understanding the Basics

At its core, lo to (also written as "lo to" or "lo to") is a Vietnamese number-matching game. A caller draws numbered tokens (1 through 90) from a bag one at a time, announcing each number aloud. Players check their lo to cards - each card contains a unique selection of numbers arranged in rows - and mark off any matching numbers. The first player to complete a full row of numbers on their card wins the round.

But what makes lo to truly special is the performance element. In traditional lo to, the caller does not simply read numbers - they sing them, often improvising rhymes, jokes, and theatrical flourishes between draws. This transforms a simple game of chance into a vibrant, interactive entertainment experience that captivates everyone in the room.

"Lo to is the game where numbers have melodies, where every draw is a mini-performance, and where the real prize is the joy of playing together." - Vietnamese folk saying

How to Play Lo To: Step-by-Step Instructions

Now for the fun part! Follow these 5 simple steps to play lo to. The rules are straightforward enough for children to learn in minutes, yet the game creates enough excitement to keep adults entertained for hours.

1

Set Up the Game & Hand Out Cards

Choose one person to be the caller (also called the "MC" or "nguoi xo so"). The caller places all 90 numbered tokens into the drawstring bag and gives it a good shake. Every other player receives one lo to card. If you are playing with a large group, players can hold multiple cards for extra challenge.

Each lo to card has 3 rows and 9 columns. Each row contains exactly 5 numbers and 4 blank spaces. The numbers are arranged so that column 1 contains numbers 1-9, column 2 contains 10-19, and so on up to column 9 which contains 80-90.

2

The Caller Draws and Announces Numbers

The caller reaches into the bag, draws out one token at a time, and announces the number to all players. In traditional Vietnamese lo to, the caller does not just say the number - they sing it, often using rhyming couplets, riddles, or humorous phrases associated with each number.

For example, the number 88 might be called as "hai cap mo" (double eyeglasses), or number 1 as "nhat phat" (first draw). The caller sets the pace of the game - a good caller builds tension by pausing dramatically before revealing each number, keeping the audience on the edge of their seats.

3

Players Mark Their Cards

As each number is called, players quickly scan their lo to cards. If a called number appears on their card, they cover it with a marker - a coin, a bean, a button, or any small object. Speed matters here! In a lively game with a fast-paced caller, you need to stay alert and mark your numbers promptly.

This is where the excitement builds. With each number called, players glance at their rows - are they getting closer to completing a line? The room fills with gasps, sighs, and excited murmurs as players edge closer (or agonisingly further) from a win.

4

Complete a Row and Shout "KINH!" (or "Lo To!")

The goal is simple: be the first player to cover all 5 numbers in any single row on your card. The moment you complete a row, you shout "KINH!" (or the traditional "Lo To!") as loudly as you can to claim your victory.

The caller then pauses the game and verifies the winning card by checking that all the marked numbers in the claimed row were actually called. If the card checks out - congratulations, you have won the round! If there is an error (a number was marked that was not called), the game continues.

5

Celebrate, Reset, and Play Again!

The winner collects their prize (if playing for prizes) or simply basks in glory. Then, clear all the markers from every card, return the tokens to the bag, shuffle and redistribute the cards if desired, and start a fresh round. Most lo to sessions involve multiple rounds - the more you play, the more fun it gets!

For added variety, you can change the winning condition: instead of one row, require two rows (called "hai hang") or even a full card (all three rows - called "lo to toan bo") for a longer, more intense game.

Lo To Rules: A Quick Summary

Need a quick reference? Here are the complete lo to rules at a glance:

  1. One player is the caller; everyone else gets a lo to card
  2. The caller draws numbered tokens (1-90) from a bag, one at a time
  3. Players mark matching numbers on their cards with markers
  4. The first player to complete a full row of 5 numbers wins
  5. The winner shouts "KINH!" (or "Lo To!") to claim the win
  6. The caller verifies the winning card against the called numbers
  7. Clear the cards, return the tokens, and play again!

Winning Conditions - Choose Your Difficulty

  • One Row (Mot Hang): Complete any single row - fastest and most common mode
  • Two Rows (Hai Hang): Complete any two rows on the same card - intermediate
  • Full Card (Lo To Toan Bo): Cover all three rows - longest and most challenging
  • Four Corners: Mark the four corner numbers on your card - a fun house rule variation
  • Diagonal: Complete a diagonal line - another popular house rule

How to Be an Amazing Lo To Caller

The caller is the heart and soul of every lo to game. A great caller transforms a simple number draw into an electrifying performance. In traditional Vietnamese lo to shows, callers are the stars - they sing, dance, joke, and keep the energy high throughout the entire game. Here is how to channel that energy at your own game night:

Tips for First-Time Callers

  1. Project your voice - make sure every player can hear every number clearly, even in a noisy room
  2. Pause for dramatic effect - do not rush through numbers; build suspense between draws
  3. Use nicknames for numbers - in Vietnamese tradition, each number has playful associations (e.g., 77 is "hai cai ong" - two walking sticks)
  4. Add music between rounds - play Vietnamese folk songs or upbeat music to keep the energy alive
  5. Interact with players - comment on near-wins, tease the audience, and celebrate with the winner
  6. Repeat each number twice - call the number, pause, then repeat to make sure everyone heard it
"A lo to game is only as good as its caller. The numbers are just an excuse - the performance is the real game."

Hosting the Perfect Lo To Game Night: Tips & Ideas

Ready to host your own lo to event? Whether it is a casual family evening, a Tet celebration, or a full-blown party, here are expert tips for creating an unforgettable lo to game night:

Setting the Scene

Traditional lo to stages are famous for their bright lights, vivid colours, and festive atmosphere. Recreate this energy at home with fairy lights or coloured LED strips, Vietnamese lanterns, and vibrant table decorations. Play Vietnamese music in the background. Set up a designated "caller's stage" area - even a simple stool or raised platform adds to the theatrical feel.

Food & Drinks

No Vietnamese gathering is complete without food! Set up a snack station with traditional treats like banh trang nuong (Vietnamese rice paper pizza), che (sweet dessert soup), fresh fruit, and Vietnamese iced coffee (ca phe sua da). Having finger foods that players can nibble between rounds keeps the energy up and makes the event feel like an authentic Vietnamese street festival.

Prizes & Rewards

While lo to is fun enough on its own, adding small prizes raises the stakes and excitement. Ideas include: Vietnamese snacks and sweets, lucky red envelopes (li xi) with small amounts of money, funny trophies, or small cultural gifts. For children's games, stickers, toys, and treats work perfectly. For adult game nights, consider novelty prizes that spark laughter.

Lo To vs Bingo: What Is the Difference?

If you are familiar with Western bingo, you already understand the basic concept of lo to. However, there are several key differences that make lo to a uniquely Vietnamese experience:

Lo To vs Bingo - Key Differences

  • Performance element: Lo to callers sing and perform; bingo callers simply read numbers
  • Number range: Lo to uses numbers 1-90; American bingo uses 1-75
  • Card layout: Lo to cards have 3 rows x 9 columns (5 numbers + 4 blanks per row); bingo cards have 5x5 grids
  • Cultural context: Lo to is deeply tied to Vietnamese festivals, community events, and street culture
  • Social atmosphere: Lo to games are louder, more theatrical, and more communal than typical bingo halls
  • Winning call: Lo to players shout "Lo To!" or "KINH!"; bingo players shout "Bingo!"

Popular Lo To Variations and House Rules

Part of lo to's enduring appeal is its flexibility. Over the centuries, communities across Vietnam have developed their own house rules and variations that add fresh twists to the classic game. Here are some popular ones to try at your next game night:

Speed Lo To

The caller draws numbers at a rapid pace with minimal pause between draws. This high-energy variation tests players' concentration and reflexes. Miss a number and you might miss your chance at winning! Perfect for experienced players who want an adrenaline rush.

Reverse Lo To

Instead of trying to complete a row, players try to avoid completing any row. The last player without a completed row wins. This clever inversion creates a completely different kind of tension - every called number that appears on your card is cause for dread rather than celebration.

Team Lo To

Players form teams of 2-4. Each team pools their cards together, and team members help each other watch for called numbers. A team wins when any of their combined cards completes a row. This variation is perfect for including younger children who can partner with older family members.

Musical Lo To

The caller sings a song between each number draw. Players must wait for the song to finish before the next number is revealed. This is the most traditional form of lo to, echoing the theatrical performances of Vietnam's travelling lo to troupes.

Pro Tip: Create Your Own House Rules!

Half the fun of lo to is making it your own. Try creating special rules for your group - maybe the winner has to perform a dance, or anyone who accidentally marks a wrong number has to sing a song. The wilder the house rules, the more memorable the game night!

Playing Lo To with Children and Families

One of lo to's greatest strengths is that it bridges generations. The game is simple enough for children as young as 6 to play, yet exciting enough to keep grandparents fully engaged. Here are tips for making lo to work for all ages:

  1. Pair young children with an older buddy who can help them find numbers on their cards
  2. Use larger markers (like bottle caps or game chips) that small hands can easily place and remove
  3. Slow down the calling pace to give everyone time to scan their cards
  4. Give every child a small prize (not just the winner) to keep the mood positive
  5. Let children take turns as the caller - they love the attention and it builds their confidence
  6. Use the game to teach number recognition - lo to is a fantastic educational tool disguised as fun

Frequently Asked Questions About Lo To

How many people can play lo to?

Lo to works with as few as 2 players and can scale up to 50 or more! The game is most fun with 6-20 players, but traditional lo to shows in Vietnam often had hundreds of players at once. With a game set like KINH! Lo To Show (which includes 20 cards), you can comfortably host up to 20 players per round.

How long does a game of lo to take?

A single round typically takes 10-20 minutes, depending on the calling speed and how quickly someone completes a row. Most game sessions involve 3-5 rounds and last about 1-2 hours total. Full-card games (lo to toan bo) can take up to 45 minutes per round.

Is lo to the same as bingo?

Lo to and bingo share the same basic concept (matching called numbers to a card), but lo to has a distinct Vietnamese cultural identity. The performance element of the caller, the card layout (3x9 vs 5x5), the number range (1-90 vs 1-75), and the vibrant social atmosphere all set lo to apart from traditional Western bingo.

What age is lo to suitable for?

Lo to is suitable for ages 6 and up. Younger children can play with help from an older partner. The game is particularly wonderful for multigenerational gatherings - it is one of the few games where grandparents, parents, and children can all participate on equal footing.
